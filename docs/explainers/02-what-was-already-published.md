# Explainer 02 — what was already published, and what we take from it

A search of the existing literature on pH-dependent binding, done 1 October 2026.
This document records what is already known, because that is what shapes the
design. Citations are traceable to `docs/decisions-log.md` under Resolved.

Replaces an earlier version of this file that spent most of its length on a claim
this project withdrew. That withdrawal is recorded once, at the end, and once is
enough.

---

## The three things that are already established

### 1. Putting histidines on a binder to make it pH-dependent

Established since 2002 and named in the literature: **histidine switching**, after
Sarkar et al. 2002. It works for the reason this project already relies on —
histidine is the only amino acid that changes charge between pH 7.4 and pH 6.5.

There is also a standard laboratory method for finding where to put them, called
**histidine scanning**: make many versions of a molecule with histidines in
different positions, measure which ones switch, keep those (Schröter et al. 2015).
Clinical-stage molecules exist that were built this way.

This is background to cite, not an idea to claim.

### 2. Putting an acidic residue on the binder against a histidine on the target

Also published, and described as a known strategy in a 2024 review (Wei & Sulea,
*mAbs* 16:2404064). It has been done deliberately, with solved structures, on at
least two other targets: CTLA-4 (Lee et al. 2022) and VISTA (Johnston et al. 2019,
taken to a clinical antibody by Thisted et al. 2024).

### 3. It has been done on EGFR, on H433, at exactly our two pH values

This is the closest piece of work to ours and the most useful one.

Liu et al. 2022, *Molecular Therapy — Oncolytics* 27:256–269
(doi:10.1016/j.omto.2022.11.001), built a pH-dependent anti-EGFR antibody. They
compared pH 6.5 against pH 7.4, required human/mouse cross-reactivity, paired an
acidic residue against EGFR's own H433, and there is a patent family around it.

Nothing about our three requirements is new ground.

## What that paper gave us, which is more than it took

Three things, all of which the current design uses.

**Which histidine to aim at, measured rather than guessed.** Liu et al. changed
each of EGFR's histidines to alanine one at a time — **alanine substitution** — and
measured which change destroyed the pH-dependent binding. Two came back: **H370**
and **H433**. Every other histidine on EGFR is, on this evidence, not worth
building a switch on. Our target histidine therefore rests on a published
measurement rather than on reasoning about which histidine looks promising.

**Which of those two to use.** H433 is the one they built on, and it sits inside
our old epitope. H370 is the other one, and no published binder uses it. Choosing
H370 was a direct consequence of reading this paper, and the measurement that
followed is in explainer 03.

**Experimental support for our rejection rule.** This project had derived, from
first principles, that you must never put a histidine on the binder facing a
histidine on the target: both go positive together at pH 6.5 and push apart, which
can cancel a correct pair elsewhere. Liu et al. tried exactly that arrangement.
Their histidine-facing-H433 version changed neither the pH dependence nor the
binding strength, while their acidic-facing-H433 versions improved pH dependence
substantially.

So a rule we reasoned our way to now has a measurement behind it. That is worth
more in a write-up than the reasoning was on its own.

## What the design now rests on

With no claim to being first, the submission still has five things behind it:

1. A target patch chosen by computing which residues are identical in human and
   mouse, so cross-reactivity follows from where we aim rather than from repair
   work later.
2. A target histidine chosen from a published experimental mapping rather than
   from reasoning about which histidine looks promising.
3. A positional charge-pairing rule with an explicit rejection criterion, which
   has published experimental support behind it.
4. An affinity target deliberately held **low**, because the requirement is a
   detection threshold and not a ratio. A weak, clearly switching binder passes
   where a strong non-switching one fails.
5. A record in which every number is regenerated by a script, and in which
   disproved assumptions are kept rather than quietly removed.

## Two things worth stating explicitly

**On the competition rules.** Using a published map of which EGFR histidines carry
pH dependence is using published information about **the target**. Starting from
Liu et al.'s **antibody** and modifying it would be starting from an existing
binder, which the rules forbid and which we are not doing. Our designs are
generated from scratch against the target surface.

**One deliberate omission.** The search also surfaced a public repository by
another entrant working on a similar approach in this competition. We did not open
it. Reading a competitor's approach would mean that any resemblance between their
designs and ours could not honestly be called independent, and the rules about
designing from scratch make that awkward to explain. Recorded here so it reads as
a choice rather than an oversight.

## The withdrawn claim

This project previously argued that pairing an acidic binder residue against the
target's own histidine was a method differentiator that standard pipelines do not
attempt. Sections 1 to 3 above show that is false, including on this target and on
this mechanism. The claim was withdrawn in full on 1 October 2026 rather than
narrowed, and no replacement novelty argument was substituted in its place.

It should have been checked before it was built on. The useful consequence is that
the search also produced the H370 finding, which is where the design went next.
