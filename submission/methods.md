# pH-conditional de novo minibinders against the H370 face of human EGFR domain III

**Anthropic × Adaptyv Protein Design Competition — Challenge 1 — Track 3 (open track)**

Single entrant, self-supported: no competition compute credits, no laboratory access.
Total compute spend on this entry is under USD 25 of rented GPU time, which makes the
method reproducible by anyone with a commodity card.

The approach was derived against the published literature rather than adapted from an
existing pH-switch pipeline, and it is documented so that each step can be checked
rather than taken on trust. A full working record exists, including every claim this
project's own computation disproved; the repository holding it is private at the time of
submission, so no link is given here rather than one that returns 404. Every number
quoted below is regenerated by a numbered script in `analysis/` and written to
`results/findings/`; none is typed in by hand.

---

## 1. Summary

We designed 48-residue de novo miniproteins intended to bind the extracellular region
of human EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor) at pH 6.5 and to release it at pH 7.4,
while binding mouse EGFR with comparable strength at pH 6.5.

The approach has four parts:

1. **Choose the epitope for cross-species identity first, by computation**, so that
   mouse cross-reactivity follows from where the binder aims rather than from repair
   work afterwards. The chosen face carries zero human/mouse differences across its
   conserved core run, residues 365–376.
2. **Build the pH switch from a positional charge-pairing rule** with an explicit
   rejection criterion, applied as a hard filter on the designed interface rather than
   as a preference. Both the target histidine we aim at and the arrangement we forbid
   come from a published experimental mapping of EGFR's pH-dependent positions (Liu et
   al. 2022), rather than from reasoning about which histidine looks promising.
   Section 5 gives that reading in full, including the claim it cost us.
3. **Target affinity deliberately low.** The competition's primary objective is a
   detection threshold at pH 7.4, which a weak switching binder can satisfy and a
   strong non-switching binder cannot.
4. **Record every attempt with its denominator**, including the ones that produced
   nothing, and withdraw claims that the project's own computation disproved.

Three of this project's own working assumptions were disproved by its own measurements
and were withdrawn rather than softened. Section 10 lists them.

**What this entry does well:** the epitope choice is computed and cross-species clean;
the mechanism is stated as a checkable per-residue rule with an experimentally
supported rejection criterion; every claim is traceable to a script.

**What it does poorly, stated plainly:** the pH switch is weakly realised. Our design
rule asks for three or more correctly paired positions at the interface. The best
design achieved two. We report this with its denominator rather than selecting
around it.

## 2. What the assay measures, and what follows

Confirmed by the organisers on 4 October 2026, two days before the deadline, and the
basis of several decisions below. Clarifications issued during the challenge were
tracked and logged as they appeared, with speaker and date, in
`docs/competition-qa-log.md`:

| | |
|---|---|
| human EGFR | tested at **both** pH 6.5 and pH 7.4 |
| mouse EGFR | tested at **pH 6.5 only** |
| constructs | full ectodomains, **tethered** form; human Met 1 – Ser 645, mouse Met 1 – Ser 647 |
| source | Sino Biological 10001-H08H (human), 51091-M08H (mouse) |
| initiator methionine | added by the organisers; not prepended by us |

Three consequences shaped the entry.

**Cross-reactivity is measured in the bound state only.** Because mouse EGFR is read at
pH 6.5, where the binder is meant to be on, the pH switch and the cross-reactivity
objective do not pull against each other. The only "off" reading is human at pH 7.4.
This makes an epitope-first strategy for cross-reactivity the right one: identity of the
contacted surface between the two species is the whole of what cross-reactivity depends
on here, and it can be computed before any design is generated.

**The tested molecule is the tethered conformation of the whole ectodomain.** Our design
work used structure 6ARU, which is the extended, open form held in complex with the
antibody cetuximab. We measured the consequence rather than assuming it away: domain III
has effectively the same fold in the extended form (6ARU) and the tethered form (1NQL),
at 1.08 Å RMSD. RMSD, root-mean-square deviation, is the average distance between two
structures' matching atoms once one is laid on the other as well as it will go; 1.08 Å
is about the width of one atom. Separately, `analysis/12` checked whether the rest of
the receptor covers our anchor face in either form, and found none of the eight anchors
contacted from outside domain III in either structure, with six fully open. The epitope
call therefore survives the conformational question. The residual risk is named in
section 11.

**The signal peptide is present in the tested protein.** The constructs begin at Met 1,
so residues 1–24 are in the test article rather than cleaved. The organisers noted that
only designs aimed near residue 25 are affected; rather than rely on that, we measured
it on our own target. In the tethered structure the four anchors our designs pair
against sit **47.2 to 61.3 Å** from the first resolved residue of the mature region,
which is the point the unmodelled leader attaches to — H358 nearest at 47.2 Å, H370
furthest at 61.3 Å. A 24-residue chain stretched out straight spans about 84 Å, so
**distance alone does not put the anchor face out of the leader's reach.** That is the
worst case and an unkind one, because a disordered chain rarely approaches full
extension and nothing here measures what this one does. The figure is also a lower
bound: no EGFR structure resolves residues 1–24, so the distance is measured to the
attachment point rather than to the leader itself. We record it as an unquantified risk
rather than as clearance (`analysis/23_signal_peptide_distance.py`).

## 3. Choosing the epitope

### 3.1 The constraint that drives everything

A binder that works on both species must contact a surface the two species share. We
therefore computed, rather than assumed, which parts of EGFR domain III are identical
between human and mouse, and chose the target face from that.

We first worked on residues 415–466, a 52-residue block with one conservative
human/mouse difference (S442G, serine to glycine) and eight charged anchor positions.
Measurement moved us off it. A conserved run at residues 365–376 centred on histidine
370 scored better on every criterion we had defined in advance:

| | 415–466 | the H370 face |
|---|---|---|
| conserved reachable anchors | 7 | **16** |
| anchors on a single face | 4 | **8** |
| target histidines available | 1 (H433) | **2** (H358, H370) |
| overlap with cetuximab's footprint | yes, at H433 | **none** |
| human/mouse differences in the core run | 1 (S442G) | **0** |
| sits in the domain III/IV interdomain groove | partly | **no** |

Cetuximab's footprint was computed from 6ARU directly, as the set of EGFR residues with
any heavy atom within 4.5 Å of either antibody Fab chain, rather than taken from a
description. Avoiding it matters because the rules forbid starting from an existing
binder, and landing on an approved antibody's exact footprint invites that objection
even for a design generated from scratch.

### 3.2 Cross-species checks on the chosen face

- **Mouse.** Zero differences across the conserved core run 365–376. All eight anchors
  on the designed face hold the same amino acid.
- **Cynomolgus macaque.** Checked because a pre-launch organiser message mentioned cyno;
  the final rules name human and mouse only. All eight anchors identical; the single
  domain III difference, S348T, is not an anchor and is the same position that also
  differs in mouse. Reported with its limits: the cyno database entry is an unreviewed
  automatic gene prediction, this is sequence analysis with no cyno structure examined,
  and nothing about binding was measured.

### 3.3 The target handed to the design run

The design run was given domain III alone, residues 310–480, 171 residues, cut from
6ARU. Two costs were measured rather than ignored:

- Cutting at 480 severs the disulfide bond between cysteines 470 and 499. A disulfide
  is a covalent staple between two cysteine side chains; severing one leaves an unpaired
  cysteine and can let the fragment's shape drift.
- Anchor E424 sits 6.6 Å from the cut edge, where the fragment's shape is least
  trustworthy. Every other anchor is 12.3 Å or further in.

We tested three boundaries — 310–480, 310–480 with the unpaired cysteine mutated to
serine, and 310–499 with the staple intact — by predicting each and comparing against
6ARU. All three came back at about 5 Å, which is the predictor's noise floor, so the
test did not separate them. We therefore kept 310–480 unchanged and did not adopt the
cysteine mutation, because a change that a measurement failed to justify is a change
made on a hunch. E424's weakness is carried as a ranking penalty rather than as an
exclusion.

## 4. The pH mechanism

### 4.1 The rule

Histidine is the amino acid whose charge changes across the range the assay spans. Its
side chain is close to neutral at pH 7.4 and largely positive at pH 6.5. Aspartate and
glutamate carry negative charge at both. The switch is built position by position
across the interface:

| position on the target | residue placed opposite it on the binder | behaviour at pH 6.5 | behaviour at pH 7.4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| acidic (Asp, Glu) | **histidine** | binder His positive, attraction | binder His neutral, attraction lost |
| histidine (H358, H370, H433) | **acidic** (Asp, Glu) | target His positive, attraction | target His neutral, attraction lost |

Every correctly paired position contributes attraction that exists at pH 6.5 and
disappears at pH 7.4. The intended behaviour is additive: more correct pairs, wider gap
between the two pH readings.

### 4.2 The rejection criterion

**A histidine on the binder facing a histidine on the target is rejected outright.**
At pH 6.5 both become positive and repel, which can cancel a correct pair elsewhere in
the same interface. We derived this from first principles before finding that it has
direct experimental support (section 5).

**A second rejection:** contact with position 442, the one human/mouse difference inside
the original epitope and a residue in cetuximab's measured contact set. This rule was
demoted during final selection; the reasoning is in section 9.

**A demotion rather than a rejection:** anchors E400, E421 and E424 are ranked below the
others, because a sugar-attachment sequence at asparagine 361 is present in human and
absent in mouse, making the local environment of those three anchors species-asymmetric
in a way the others are not. Reported honestly: this asymmetry is between human and
mouse specifically; the cyno sequence has the N361 site.

### 4.3 Affinity is targeted low on purpose

The primary objective is phrased as a threshold — no detectable binding at pH 7.4 —
rather than as a ratio. A binder strong enough to remain detectable at pH 7.4 fails that
objective however large its fold-change. A weak binder that is clearly off at pH 7.4
passes it. Our ranking therefore leads on charge-pair count and uses the pipeline's own
interface-confidence score only as a tie-break, with no term anywhere that rewards
higher predicted affinity.

## 5. Relation to published work

We searched the literature before building, and the search overturned this project's
original novelty claim. We record that here rather than quietly dropping it.

**Liu et al. 2022** (*Molecular Therapy — Oncolytics* 27:256–269) built a pH-dependent
anti-EGFR antibody by pairing an acidic residue against EGFR's own histidine 433. It is
cross-reactive and patented. Histidine switching as a technique dates to 2002. Our
original claim — that pairing an acidic binder residue against a target histidine was a
differentiator — was therefore false, including on this exact target and mechanism. It
was **withdrawn in full on 1 October 2026**, and no substitute novelty claim was put in
its place.

Two things we take from that paper, both of which strengthen the entry:

- **Our target histidine rests on their experimental mapping rather than on our
  judgement.** Their histidine scan across EGFR found exactly two positions carrying pH
  dependence: H370 and H433. We chose H370, the one no published binder uses, and that
  choice was a direct consequence of reading their result.
- **Our rejection criterion has a measurement behind it.** They tried the arrangement we
  forbid — a histidine on the binder facing H433 — and it changed neither the pH
  dependence nor the binding strength, while their acidic-facing-H433 constructs improved
  pH dependence substantially.

**Proton-PottsMPNN** (Jacobsen et al., bioRxiv 2026.09.30.755438) was released during
this challenge and highlighted to all entrants by the organisers on 4 October. It treats
the protonated and deprotonated states of histidine, aspartate and glutamate as distinct
sequence tokens, so protonation preference is specified inside the design model's energy
function. Its own framing of the field is accurate about our method as well: where the
design model has no representation of protonation state, pH sensitivity ends up
**screened for rather than specified**.

Our method is in that screened-for category, and we state it rather than obscure it. We
did not use Proton-PottsMPNN: it appeared four days before the deadline, and this entry's
remaining budget was about USD 10 of GPU time, which does not cover standing up and
validating a second design stack. What our approach offers instead is a different
trade-off, and it is the one we would defend:

- The rule is **explicit and checkable at the level of individual residue pairs**. For
  any submitted design we can state which binder residue faces which target residue, what
  each is expected to do at each pH, and which rule admitted or rejected it.
- It carries a **falsifiable rejection criterion with published experimental support**,
  so the filter removes designs for a stated reason rather than on a score.
- It runs on commodity hardware at about USD 0.28 per design attempt.

**De novo pH-conditional binder design against EGFR specifically.** Our searching found
pH-dependent anti-EGFR molecules in the literature only as antibodies, and de novo
pH-switchable miniprotein design demonstrated on other targets (Ahn et al. 2025, bioRxiv
2025.09.29.678932, switching in the opposite direction; Proton-PottsMPNN on PD-L1). We
state this as "we found no published example", not as "this has never been done".

## 6. The design pipeline

**Generator.** BindCraft2 v1.0.1, run on Modal against an NVIDIA L4 GPU. BindCraft2
generates a binder backbone against the target by gradient-based hallucination, then
designs sequences onto it with ProteinMPNN, then re-predicts and ranks the complexes.

**Target.** The 171-residue domain III fragment above, with four hotspot residues given
to the generator: E344, H358, D368, H370. These are the four anchors on the single best
face by span and by species conservation, and they include both available target
histidines.

**Off-target counter-selection.** The assay immobilises the target through an affinity
tag. A binder that grips the tag rather than EGFR would read as a hit and mean nothing.
We supplied the tag sequence to the generator as a declared off-target so the design
loop actively avoids it, rather than screening for it afterwards.

**Numbering guard.** Every residue number in this project is a position in the full human
UniProt record P00533, where the mature ectodomain runs 25–645 and the challenge
construct's position 1 is our position 25. Structure files carry their own numbering and
nothing in the file records which convention it uses, so a mix-up shifts every result by
24 with no error raised. A dedicated check compares the target chain of every returned
complex against the input, and the scoring step refuses to score any candidate that fails
it. This caught a real fault in an earlier run.

**The added initiator methionine.** The organisers prepend a methionine to every
submitted sequence and ours begin with serine or aspartate, so each tested protein
carries one residue we did not design, at the N-terminus. BindCraft2's own
`N_Terminus_Away_Cosine` reports how far that terminus points away from the interface,
where +1 is directly away and 0 is perpendicular. Across the seven submitted designs it
runs **−0.10 to +0.11**: the N-terminus is side-on to the target rather than pointing
away from it, and on three designs it tilts very slightly towards it. The C-terminus,
which the design loop was explicitly told to keep clear because the immobilisation tail
attaches there, reads 0.67 to 0.82 and is genuinely directed away. So the added
methionine sits at an end that is not shielded from the interface, and we have no
measurement of what it does. We state it rather than assume it is harmless.

**Confidence floors.** BindCraft2's internal interface-confidence floors were lowered
from 0.5 to 0.35 at the middle stages and from 0.7 to 0.5 at the final stage. Stated
plainly: these are designs the predictor does not confidently believe bind. The ground
for lowering them is that BindCraft2's defaults are its own conservative setting rather
than this competition's bar, the organisers have said that in-silico scoring is a smaller
component of selection and that worse confidence on a rigid or obstructed epitope will
not be penalised much, and our own ranking leads on charge-pair count rather than on
confidence. We regard this as the weakest methodological choice in the entry.

## 7. Screens applied to every candidate

Four screens run on every design. Each was built and self-tested **before** any candidate
existed, by constructing inputs that must fail and confirming they do — a check never
seen to fail is not known to work.

| screen | what it asks | outcome on the ten candidates |
|---|---|---|
| **charge-pair filter** | which contacts obey the pairing rule; does any binder His face a target His; is 442 contacted | 0 of 10 reach three correct pairs; best is two |
| **full-receptor clash** | superposed back into the intact receptor, does the binder occupy space another domain already fills | 9 clear, 1 clashing (one hard overlap at 1.96 Å) |
| **tag counter-screen** | does the binder engage the affinity tag | all 10 pass comfortably: 0.08–0.11 against a 0.4 ceiling |
| **novelty** | sequence similarity to known proteins | no significant similarity to anything in Swiss-Prot |

**On the novelty result specifically.** MMseqs2 (version 18-8cc5c, sensitivity 7.5)
against the Swiss-Prot release of 3 October 2026, 575,748 sequences. Raw percent
identities run 31–42%, which looks alarming and is not: across 2,239 hits the best
e-value is 0.30 and none reaches 0.001. A 48-residue query finds 35–40% identity over 40
residues by chance routinely in a database this size. We state the limits as well: this
searched Swiss-Prot only, not the Protein Data Bank, the patent databases, THPdb,
PLAbDab or Proteinbase; nothing structural was run; and all ten sequences are
redesigns of one backbone, so this is one backbone checked rather than ten.

## 8. Results, with denominators

Every design attempt is recorded, including the ones that produced nothing. Rates are
quoted per configuration with the denominator attached, because pooling configurations
that were testing different things produces a misleading single number.

| configuration | attempts | reached the final stage | candidates written |
|---|---|---|---|
| eight hotspots, various diagnostics | 20 | 0 | 0 |
| four hotspots (the set above) | 6 | 2 | 0 |
| round one, binder length 30–60 | 10 | 1 | **10** |
| round one, binder length 60–100 | 10 | 0 | 0 |

Round one's two denominators were recorded as 9 per band on 3 October 2026, from a
version of our ledger script that was fixed at 22:48 and 22:50 that night. They were
recomputed from the raw trajectory records during submission verification and are 10 per
band, which moved five cost figures below.

**The eight-hotspot face does not work and the four-hotspot one does.** That cost 26
attempts to establish and it is the finding everything downstream rests on. The
comparison is suggestive rather than controlled: those campaigns differed in more than
hotspot count.

**Long binders do not find this face and short ones sometimes do.** Across ten attempts
each, the 60–100 residue band wrote no structure at any stage, with interface confidence
during optimisation running 0.21–0.25 against the 30–60 band's 0.73–0.83. On ten against
ten this is more than a one-event margin. It is why the submitted designs are 48 residues
and sit in the minibinder category.

**The charge-pair survival rate now has a denominator, and it is poor.** Of ten
candidates, none reaches three correct pairs; the best two reach two each. All ten
descend from a single successful trajectory, so this is one backbone's answer repeated
ten times — weaker evidence than it sounds, in both directions.

**Measured costs:** USD 0.281 of GPU card time per attempt in the short band and
USD 0.332 in the long band, and USD 0.281 per candidate written. Billed cost runs higher
than card time, because the compute provider also charges for container start-up and
processor time: the two bands were billed USD 3.16 and USD 3.72 against USD 2.81 and
USD 3.32 of card time. Peak GPU memory 8,894 MiB in the short band against 17,227 MiB in
the long band, on a card with 23,034 MiB.

## 9. The submitted set, and why each slot is spent that way

### 9.1 Ranking criterion

Designs are ranked on four terms, in order: the number of correctly paired positions;
how many of those pairs sit within reach of the charge groups involved; the total number
of residue-residue contacts the design makes with the target, at a 4.5 Å heavy-atom
cutoff; and the pipeline's own interface-confidence score as a final tie-break. No term
anywhere rewards higher predicted affinity.

The third term exists because of an effect worth naming: **a design can pass every hard
rejection rule by making almost no contact at all.** Four of the ten candidates make
between one and three residue-residue contacts with the target in total. They break no
rule because there is nothing there to break one. Ranking on rule compliance alone would
have put near-non-binders above the designs that genuinely engage the face, and an
earlier draft of this submission did exactly that.

The second term is what separates the top two designs, which carry two correct pairs
each: one has both of its pairs within reach, the other has one.

### 9.2 One rule demoted, deliberately

Our two best designs by charge-pair count both contact position 442, which our own rule
rejected. We demoted that rule to a ranking penalty for this submission, on three stated
grounds:

1. The organisers rank **pH selectivity above cross-reactivity**. A rule protecting the
   second objective should not silently remove the only designs that serve the first.
2. S442G is a conservative substitution, serine to glycine, and it is one position among
   the 31 and 39 residue-residue contacts those two designs make. The risk is a shift in the
   mouse-to-human affinity ratio rather than a loss of binding.
3. The project already handles comparable risks this way: anchor E424's proximity to the
   fragment's cut edge is a ranking penalty, and the clash screen flags rather than drops.
   Demoting 442 makes the treatment consistent.

This is a judgement, and we flag it as one. Both affected designs are labelled in the
table below so the trade-off is visible rather than buried.

### 9.3 Two rules kept

- **Designs whose binder histidine faces target histidine H433 are excluded.** This is
  the arrangement Liu et al. measured and found inert. Submitting it would spend slots on
  an arrangement with published evidence against it.
- **The design that clashes with the intact receptor is excluded**, on one hard overlap
  at 1.96 Å near residue 489. This exclusion became stronger, not weaker, once the
  organisers confirmed the test article is the full tethered ectodomain rather than a
  fragment.

### 9.4 The designs

All are 48 residues, molecule class `protein`, in the **minibinder** category (40–100
residues).

| rank | name | correct pairs | contacts made (4.5 Å) | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | egfr-ph-h370-01 | 2 | 31 | contacts 442 — see 9.2 |
| 2 | egfr-ph-h370-02 | 2 | 39 | contacts 442 — see 9.2 |
| 3 | egfr-ph-h370-03 | 1 (at H433) | 19 | clean on every rule |
| 4 | egfr-ph-h370-04 | 1 | 3 | thin interface |
| 5 | egfr-ph-h370-05 | 0 | 3 | thin interface |
| 6 | egfr-ph-h370-06 | 0 | 1 | thin interface |
| 7 | egfr-ph-h370-07 | 0 | 1 | thin interface |

**On submitting seven rather than twenty.** All seven descend from one backbone, and
their pairwise sequence identity runs 73% to 94% with a mean of 81%. Additional slots
filled from the same source would add sequence variation without adding a second idea.
We would rather submit a set we can describe honestly than fill the allowance. Where further backbones were obtained before the deadline they are added and
this table is regenerated; the ranking rule above is unchanged by their arrival.

## 10. Claims this project tested and withdrew

Recorded because a design record that only contains its successes cannot be checked.

1. **That pairing an acidic binder residue against a target histidine was a method
   differentiator.** Disproved by the literature search; withdrawn in full, not narrowed,
   on 1 October 2026.
2. **That four named human/mouse differences (Q390R, E412D, R414W, K467R) explain
   cetuximab's failure on mouse EGFR.** This was carried from memory. Computing
   cetuximab's real contact set from 6ARU settled it by measurement rather than leaving
   it as an assumption.
3. **That the fragment's low target-confidence score came from a filter meant for
   sequence-supplied targets.** The argument named the wrong metric: the setting invoked
   governs a different quantity from the one the rejections reported. The diagnostic built
   on it tested nothing, and the campaign it was attached to is reported as what it
   actually was — ten attempts at default settings.
4. **That the design tool held our card to a single design worker because it caps
   fan-out at the trajectory budget.** Carried in our own log for two campaigns and
   disproved on 5 October 2026 by reading the tool's source. The worker count is clamped
   by an estimate of card memory per worker that carries a safety multiplier of 2.0; for
   a complex of our size it budgets 11.78 GB and so permits one worker on a 22.49 GB
   card, whatever worker count is requested. The trajectory budget was never what bound
   it. Our own measured peak is 8.69 GB a worker, so the card holds two and the estimate
   is what refused them. Recalibrating that estimate at run time, from outside the tool
   rather than by editing it, ran the final campaign on two workers; the throughput that
   bought is reported with its denominator in section 8.

Two further corrections are in the full log: a disagreement between two scripts computing
the same contact set from the same file, and an automated check that reported a confident
wrong conclusion because its input list swept up the tool's own template structures.

## 11. Risks we have not resolved

Stated so a reader is not left to find them.

- **Conformation.** Designs were shaped against domain III cut from the extended form
  (6ARU); the assay uses the tethered ectodomain. Domain III's fold is nearly identical
  between the two (1.08 Å) and the anchor face is unoccluded in both, so the epitope call
  holds. What is unverified is whether a fragment cut from the tethered structure would
  have produced different designs.
- **Interface confidence.** The submitted designs were generated under lowered confidence
  floors. The predictor does not confidently believe they bind.
- **One backbone.** All submitted designs descend from a single successful trajectory.
  Pairwise sequence identity within the submitted set runs 73% to 94%, mean 81%.
  Structural diversity within this submission is low, and we give the number rather than
  letting sequence variation imply otherwise.
- **The switch is weak.** Two correct pairs against a target of three. Whether two pairs
  produce a measurable difference between pH 6.5 and pH 7.4 is exactly what the experiment
  would answer and what no computation here can.
- **Sugar chains.** The attachment site at asparagine 444 lies within 25 Å of two anchors.
  No anchor is within 5 Å of any sugar atom resolved in the structure, which is a weaker
  statement than it looks, because the structure resolves only the first sugars of a chain
  that is longer in the real protein.
- **Buried-charge energetics.** Our pairing rule counts correct pairs and does not model
  how desolvation changes the pKa of the residues involved. A histidine's actual
  switching midpoint depends on its environment, and that is the quantity a method like
  Proton-PottsMPNN represents and ours does not.
- **Structural novelty: run, and it is the half that decides our level.** Each submitted
  design's predicted structure was searched against the Protein Data Bank with FoldSeek
  and aligned with TM-align. All seven are covered 94–100% by a known fold at TM-scores
  of **0.64 to 0.78**, which is moderate structural similarity on the organisers' scale:
  above the 0.5 line, below the 0.8 that would make it high. With no significant sequence
  homology, exactly one of the two level-3 conditions is met, so **all seven score level
  3 and clear the gate** (`analysis/24_structural_novelty.py`).

  Two qualifications, both load-bearing. First, designs 05 and 07 sit at TM 0.767 and
  0.783, within 0.05 of the 0.8 line that would make the similarity high and drop them to
  level 2; a different alignment could land them the other side. Second, the level rests
  on reading our sequence similarity as at or below 30%. Raw identities against Swiss-Prot
  run 31–42%, but every one is statistically insignificant — best e-value 0.30 across
  2,239 hits, none below 0.001 — and FoldSeek's own identities over these structural
  alignments are 0.9% to 11.5%. Read the other way, all seven would be level 2. The
  platform scores novelty automatically on upload and that score is the one that counts.

## 12. Reproducibility

- Residue numbering is one convention throughout, asserted by a script that stops the run
  if it breaks.
- Any quantity needed by two scripts is computed in one place, after two scripts computing
  the same contact set disagreed.
- Everything under `results/` and `data/derived/` is script output and is never
  hand-edited; prose may quote those numbers and must trace to them.
- Every design attempt, including those that produced nothing, is recorded with its
  outcome, the gate that stopped it and a file-level fingerprint, so acceptance rates can
  be recomputed by anyone.
- Each phase has a plain-language walkthrough that names the technical term for every
  measurement alongside what it measures, so a reader who was not present for the work
  can audit the reasoning rather than only the code.

For every submitted design, which binder residue faces which target

Generated by `analysis/22_pair_appendix.py` from
`data/derived/10-candidate-pairs.csv`, so it cannot drift from what was

Histidine is the only amino acid whose charge changes across this
